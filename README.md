# Cooking Equipment

## Knives & Cutting Tools

* **Good Knives:** A standard chef knife and a *Mezzaluna* (half-moon), as well as any other knives that you are skilled with. You probably want a few paring knives in your inventory. Always make sure to choose a knife that suits you.
* **Cutting Board:** Essential for protecting your surfaces and blades.
* **Utility Tools:** Also make sure you have kitchen scissors and can openers.

## Cookware & Vessels

* **Pots & Pans:** Earthenware cooking pots/pans and metal cooking pots/pans are the choice of superior chefs.
  > **Note on Non-Stick Cookware:** Non-stick coated pots/pans may be used, but they are neither as durable nor as healthy, because the coating comes off and gets into the food. When the coating gets destroyed, the non-stick pan becomes useless and must be thrown away. For this reason, using non-stick pans is generally discouraged unless a recipe specifically requires it.
* **Specialty Pans:** A nice wok pan.
* **Covers:** Your pans should have covers, and you should ideally be able to interchange the same size cover across all your pots and pans.
* **Mixing Bowls:** Several mixing bowls, preferably metal.

## Appliances & Processing Tools

* **Food Processor or Food Mill:** Extremely useful for processing staples such as garlic and mashed potatoes—there is no need to manually peel your potatoes or garlic if you have one of these inexpensive devices. Food mills have countless uses; for instance, in an Italian kitchen, a food mill can turn tomatoes into tomato puree by separating the puree from the skin.
* **Blender:** An extremely useful tool for Mexican food and for making drinks.

## Utensils & Prep Essentials

* **Wooden/Bamboo Spoons & Spatulas:** Very important for cooking nearly any dish. These are cheap and expendable, though they do not take well to being machine-washed and can develop mold easily.
  * *Alternative:* If mold is a serious problem, you may want plastic utensils instead. Plastic spoons and spatulas are easier to wash and much more resistant to mold, though they aren't used by superior chefs due to their tendency to melt under high heat (which wood doesn't do).
* **Colander & Sieve:** Crucial for draining everything from rice to pasta. Ensure you clean the colander thoroughly and often.
* **Measuring Cups:** Help a lot with accuracy. While they aren't required once you memorize a recipe, measuring cups are necessary to reproduce a recipe consistently and help with tuning a dish to perfection. While many elite chefs don't use measuring cups, they rely on other convenient measurement methods; when you are trying a new recipe, a measuring cup is pretty indispensable.
* **Parchment Paper:** A must-have for baking and non-stick applications such as croissants and chocolate chip cookies.
* **Cheesecloth:** Required for straining certain things such as broth and cheese.
