# Bruschetta

Bruschetta is an Italian name for grilled garlic bread. The lion's share of this recipe has been taken from Chef Joe Famularo's book *A Cook's Tour of Italy*. Once considered a poor man's dish, this basic recipe is a staple of Italian and European cooking. K0K0SH@'s recipe is a more "garlicky" version of Chef Famularo's. For your convenience, several methods for adding the garlic to the Bruschetta are provided.

> **Equipment Note:** In order to do this recipe, Chef Famularo says you need a grill, a broiler, or an oven with broil functionality. However, I've seen chefs make Bruschetta with other types of ovens, including just standard ones.

## Ingredients

* **Bread Slices:** Preferably Italian bread cut 1-inch thick. Chef Famularo says whole-grain is preferred, but white is fine too.

* **Garlic**

* **Olive Oil**

* **Coarse Salt**

* **Black Pepper**

* *Optional:* Balsamic Vinegar

## Instructions

1. **Prepare the garlic** to be spread on the bread later:

   * **For a milder garlic taste:** Take a clove and cut it in half. Rubbing the cut cloves on the bread once it is cooked is sufficient (this is Chef Famularo's method).

   * **For a more pronounced garlic taste (Recommended):** Use a food mill, food processor, or knife to mince the garlic into tiny pieces, and place it in a small container.

   * **K0K0SH@'s Preferred Method:** Mix the minced garlic directly with the olive oil on a flat dish or saucer rather than a bowl—you only need the oil and garlic mixture to be about 1/4 inch deep. You may optionally add a little balsamic vinegar to this mix.

2. **Preheat** the grill or broiler.

3. **Grill both sides** of each piece of bread until golden brown and crispy, flipping the slices halfway through. *(Note: If your oven cooks evenly on both sides, flipping isn't required. Do not overcook the bruschetta.)*

4. **Apply the garlic:** Take the garlic (or garlic-oil mix) prepared in Step 1—using a spoon will probably make this much easier—and rub or spread it onto the bruschetta, preferably on the side that was cooked last (if applicable).

5. **Add olive oil:** If you haven't already combined the garlic and olive oil, spoon or brush the olive oil onto the bread now, or simply dip the bruschetta directly into the oil on the dish.

6. **Season** with coarse salt and black pepper to taste.

7. **Serve** the bruschetta by itself (perhaps with a small bowl of olive oil served alongside for dipping), or top with your favorite bread spreads such as cheese, caviar, sauce, guacamole, meat, or any combination of toppings you like with toast. *(Extra butter is probably not needed because of the olive oil.)*
